DOC_ID: 111

Requires users to uphold guiding principles such as effectiveness, transparency, equity, accountability, privacy, and security.

Prohibits AI applications involving real-time biometric identification, emotion analysis, fully automated impactful decisions, social scoring, and cognitive manipulation.

Mandates reporting AI-caused harm within 24 hours.

Implements sunset procedures for outdated AI systems.

Obliges compliance with public records laws and City’s transparency policies.

Enforces policy adherence, with violations subject to disciplinary action.

Requires Finance to obtain AI usage disclosures in procurement.

Defines AI and related system terms within the policy context.

Mandates covered U.S. persons to respond to BIS inquiries within 30 days and correct incomplete submissions within 14 days.

DOC_ID: 115
Enacts the Preventing Algorithmic Discrimination Act in Illinois to combat discrimination by automated decision tools. 

Defines "algorithmic discrimination" as resulting from AI tool usage that causes discrimination against protected classes.

Requires deployers to perform annual impact assessments on automated tools by January 2027, analyzing potential biases and preventing discrimination. 

Mandates notifications to individuals affected by AI decisions, with options for alternative processes. 

Requires an alternative process for those that request AI tools not be used in a decision.

Obliges deployers to establish comprehensive governance programs to manage discrimination risks, requiring technical safeguards and annual compliance reviews. 

Mandates public availability of policies on AI tool usage and discrimination management. 

Prohibits use of tools resulting in algorithmic discrimination, with civil actions allowed from January 2028 for violations, including compensatory damages and legal fees. 

Obliges deployers to submit impact assessments to the Attorney General, with fines for non-compliance. 

Integrates enforcement with the Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act.

DOC_ID: 117

Defines "artificial intelligence literacy" as understanding basic AI principles, applications, implications, limitations, and ethical considerations.

Requires grants to support AI literacy initiatives in public elementary and secondary schools, including teacher training, professional development, and resource development.

Supports higher education institutions to develop AI learning labs, virtual platforms, and educational tools for teachers and community educators.

Includes grants to community anchor institutions for AI education and training programs.

Mandates an annual report to Congress summarizing and analyzing the progress and impact of AI literacy initiatives, with recommendations for expansion or modification.

DOC_ID: 118
Requires the inclusion of disclaimers on political advertisements containing content substantially generated by AI.

Mandates that disclaimers must clearly state the use of AI in creating or materially altering the content.

Establishes safe harbor guidelines for what constitutes a "clear and conspicuous manner" in disclaimers for image, audio, and video communications.

Amends the Federal Election Campaign Act of 1971 to include penalties for violations of the disclaimer requirements, with fines up to $50,000.

Directs the Federal Election Commission (FEC) to issue regulations outlining criteria for determining substantially AI-generated content and requirements for the disclosure statement. 

Requires the FEC to report on compliance and enforcement within two years and biannually thereafter to Congress.

DOC_ID: 119
Prohibits insurers from using external consumer data, algorithms, and predictive models that result in unfair discrimination based on various protected characteristics such as race, gender, or sexual orientation.

Requires the Commissioner of Insurance to adopt rules ensuring insurers demonstrate their data and models do not result in unfair discrimination.

Engages a stakeholder process to gather input from insurers, consumers, and other parties before rule adoption.

Obliges insurers to provide detailed information about their data usage and implement risk management frameworks to minimize discrimination risks.

Allows the Commissioner to examine and investigate insurers' data practices.

Permits exemption for certain insurance types such as title insurance and high-premium commercial insurance policies.

Defines key terms like "algorithm" and "predictive model" as related to insurance practices, emphasizing consumer data's impact on discrimination.
