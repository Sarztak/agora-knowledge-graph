DOC_ID: 490
Prohibits generative AI ("deep synthesis") systems in China from producing a variety of forms of unwanted content, including false information or content that harms lawful rights, national security and "social order."

Requires deep synthesis systems to verify the true identity of each of their users.

Requires deep synthesis developers to assess the security of their AI systems, including by assessing the systems' cybersecurity attributes and their ability to abide by relevant regulations.

Requires deep synthesis systems to notify any individuals whose biometric information is edited in the production of AI-generated content.

Requires the labelling or watermarking  of realistic deep synthesis content that could potentially mislead or confuse the public.

Requires deep synthesis developers to submit filings with the relevant authorities, and requires regulators to carry out inspections of deep synthesis systems.

DOC_ID: 491
Mandates the imposition of sanctions on the People's Republic of China if the People’s Liberation Army initiates a military invasion of Taiwan. 

Requires sanctions against Chinese officials and entities involved in military campaigns or efforts undermining Taiwan's security and infrastructure.

Blocks property and imposes visa restrictions on identified Chinese officials and affiliates involved in military and coercive actions against Taiwan.

Prohibits U.S. financial institutions from investments that benefit Chinese government-controlled entities or priority sectors like artificial intelligence and other advanced technologies.

Bans the listing and trading of securities of Chinese entities on U.S. securities exchanges following an invasion.

Prohibits the importation of goods from China post-invasion unless exceptions for national security are made.

Grants the President authority to enforce sanctions and provides for penalties under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.

DOC_ID: 492
Articulates characteristics of trustworthy AI, including validity, reliability, safety, security, accountability, explainability, privacy enhancement, and fairness in managing harmful bias. 

Encourages a balance of these characteristics based on AI systems' context of use, noting tradeoffs such as interpretability versus privacy. 

Emphasizes the importance of human judgment in deciding metrics and thresholds for AI trustworthiness. 

Stipulates that trustworthy AI depends on social and organizational behavior, datasets, model choices, and human interactions. 

Mandates addressing AI risks through ongoing testing, monitoring, and balancing accuracy and robustness. 

Calls for clear information and responsible decision-making to ensure safe AI operation. 

Advocates for secure and resilient AI systems that withstand adverse events and protect against unauthorized access. 

Promotes transparency and accountability to enhance confidence in AI systems, with transparency enabling actionable redress. 

Urges explainability and interpretability to improve understanding of AI system operations and outputs. 

Encourages privacy-enhancing technologies to safeguard autonomy and identity, acknowledging tradeoffs with accuracy. 

Highlights fairness in AI, addressing harmful bias and discrimination, and recognizing systemic, computational, and human-cognitive biases. 

Outlines AI risk management framework (AI RMF) functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE, emphasizing a continuous and iterative process. 

Specifies GOVERN function for cultivating risk management culture, policies, and accountability in AI development and deployment. 

Describes MAP function for establishing context and understanding AI system impacts to inform risk management decisions. 

Details MEASURE function for using tools and methodologies to assess AI risks and system trustworthiness. 

Explains MANAGE function for prioritizing and responding to AI risks, maximizing benefits, and managing third-party risks.

DOC_ID: 494
Requires content generated by AI to include clear and conspicuous disclosures marking content as AI-generated. 

Mandates the disclosure to identify content as AI-generated, the tool used to create output, and the creation date and time, ensuring disclosures are permanent or not easily removed.

Compels developers and third-party licensees of generative AI systems to implement procedures preventing content distribution without disclosures, including contractual obligations and certifying compliance.

Treats violations as unfair or deceptive acts under the Federal Trade Commission Act, granting enforcement powers to the FTC.

Establishes a working group to develop AI-generated content detection standards and assist platforms in identifying AI-generated content.

Requires the working group to publish a framework with guidelines and submit a report to Congress recommending legislative or administrative actions.

DOC_ID: 495
Requires developers of high-risk AI systems to disclose foreseeable uses and risks of their systems, the inputs used in their training data, and how the systems were evaluated for performance.

Requires developers to display these disclosures publicly on their websites in a public use case inventory.

Mandates developers to disclose to the Colorado Attorney General, within 90 days, if their systems have caused or will likely cause algorithmic discrimination.

Requires deployers of high-risk AI systems to notify consumers of how AI is used to make consequential decisions and inform them of their right to opt out of the processing of their personal data.

Requires deployers to give consumers the opportunity to correct erroneous personal data and appeal adverse consequential decisions.

Mandates deployers to make all these disclosures publicly available on their websites.

Provides exemptions to the Colorado AI Act for certain deployers of high-risk AI systems including those who employ less than 50 people and do not use thier own data to train the system.

Grants the Colorado Attorney General exclusive authority to enforce all regulations laid out by this act and engage in any additional rulemaking necessary for the implementation and enforcement of this act.

DOC_ID: 497
Directs the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) to mandate impact assessments of automated decision systems, especially in critical areas like employment, education, and healthcare.

Requires covered entities to conduct ongoing evaluations of their systems, including privacy, performance, and potential negative impacts on consumers.

Mandates transparency and stakeholder consultation in system development and deployment.

Obligates covered entities to remedy any material negative impacts on consumers.

Establishes guidelines for identifying covered entities, prioritizing impact assessments, and reporting findings in a machine-readable format.

Develops a publicly accessible repository for summaries of impact assessments to aid consumer protection and promote research.

Enforces compliance under the FTC Act and allows state attorneys general to take legal action.

Establishes a Bureau of Technology within the FTC to support implementation and enforcement.

Preserves state laws and coordination with federal agencies to ensure consistent regulatory practices.

DOC_ID: 499
Mandates the Secretary of Agriculture, in coordination with the National Institute of Standards and Technology and the Federal Communications Commission, to develop voluntary interconnectivity standards for precision agriculture within two years.

Requires these standards to address the evolving demands and connectivity needs of precision agriculture and consider cybersecurity challenges and the impact of AI and advanced wireless technology.

Obligates the Secretary to collaborate with relevant stakeholders, including public and private sector organizations and local governments, when developing these standards.

Directs the Comptroller General to conduct a study one year after the development of these standards and biennially thereafter for eight years, assessing the voluntary nature of the standards and their effectiveness in encouraging the adoption of precision agriculture.

Requires the findings of these studies to be reported to relevant Congressional committees.
