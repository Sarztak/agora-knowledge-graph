DOC_ID: 74
Mandates Parties to adopt measures ensuring AI systems align with human rights, democracy, and the rule of law.

Requires transparency, accountability, and non-discrimination in AI systems' lifecycle.

Obligates implementation of measures to protect privacy, human dignity, and democratic processes.

Demands risk assessment and mitigation strategies for AI, considering human rights impacts.

Establishes a follow-up mechanism for international cooperation and compliance monitoring.

Provides for accessible remedies for human rights violations arising from AI systems.

Calls for public consultation and digital literacy to address AI-related impacts.

Allows exceptions for artificial intelligence systems related to the protection of national security interests but insists on adherence to international human rights law and democratic processes.

Facilitates international cooperation and information exchange on AI's human rights impacts.

Permits amendments and dispute settlements through negotiation or the Conference of the Parties.

DOC_ID: 75
Regulates the use of facial recognition technology for processing facial information within China, ensuring compliance with relevant laws and ethical standards.

Exempts the use of facial recognition in research and development or algorithm training.

Requires a specific, necessary purpose for processing, with minimized impact on individual rights.

Obligates personal information processors to inform individuals transparently about processing activities, including purposes, methods, and rights.

Mandates obtaining consent separately for processing, with provision for withdrawal.

Requires parental consent for processing minors' facial information and formulating specific rules for handling such data.

Restricts storing facial information within devices unless consent or laws allow external transmission and limits retention to what is necessary.

Mandates conducting personal information protection impact assessments in advance and retaining assessment reports for three years.

Prohibits using facial recognition as the sole identity verification method if alternatives exist.

Requires filing procedures with local cyberspace administrations if information volume reaches 100,000 individuals.

Empowers cyberspace administrations and public security authorities to supervise, inspect, and handle complaints regarding facial recognition misuse.

DOC_ID: 77
Mandates that employers or employment agencies conduct a bias audit of Automated Employment Decision Tools (AEDTs) at least annually.

Requires bias audits to calculate selection and scoring rates, and impact ratios across sex, race/ethnicity, and intersectional categories.

Mandates AEDT users to publish bias audit summaries online, detailing data sources and impact ratios, accessible for at least 6 months after each AEDT use.

Requires notification to candidates and employees 10 business days before AEDT use, offering options via website, job postings, mail, or email.

Enforces online publication of AEDT data retention policies and types of data collected.

DOC_ID: 78
Establishes basic safety requirements for generative AI services, focusing on training data safety, model safety, and safety measures.

Requires service providers to conduct safety assessments and manage training data sources, ensuring data does not contain over 5% illegal content.

Mandates the verification of intellectual property rights and personal information consent before using data for training.

Stipulates regular security audits and monitoring of models to prevent malicious attacks and ensure content accuracy.

Obliges disclosure of service limitations, models, and personal data usage to users.

Demands the implementation of mechanisms for complaints and a backup strategy for service continuity.

Sets specific guidelines for minors, requiring parental controls and restrictions on unsuitable content.

Requires transparency in service provision, detailing applicable scenarios and user consent for data use in training.

Mandates service providers to construct banks of unsafe key words and questions which AI models should and should not refuse to answer.

DOC_ID: 79
Sets the Ministry of Defence (MOD) Responsible Officers to ensure the ethical and safe development and use of AI in Defence.

Requires MOD staff to implement this JSP in AI system lifecycles, from inception to disposal, ensuring alignment with MOD's AI Ethical Principles: Human-centricity, Responsibility, Understanding, Bias and Harm Mitigation, and Reliability.

Mandates TLB Holders and Chief Executives to issue appropriate AI-related directions and ensure competent management within their areas and maintaining Governance as outlined in Section 4.

Stresses the need for a holistic approach aligned with the UK National AI Strategy and addresses unique AI security risks, advocating continuous security monitoring.

Obliges the assessment and mitigation of ethical risks, providing evidence of responsible AI use, and addressing novel and contentious AI applications with special attention.

Emphasizes human collaboration with AI, requiring competency assessments, training, and ethical governance, particularly in human/AI teams, AI legal considerations, and supplier competence and conformance with AI-specific requirements.
