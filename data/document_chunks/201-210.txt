DOC_ID: 201
Establishes the "New York AI Act" to ensure AI development and use in New York State is safe, responsible and non-discriminatory.

Requires developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems to undergo independent audits for algorithmic discrimination before and after deployment.

Mandates detailed reporting by developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems to the Attorney General, covering system capabilities and potential risks.

Prohibits the deployment of AI causing algorithmic discrimination or of systems that have not passed the mandated audit.

Establishes whistleblower protections for employees disclosing AI-related non-compliance.

Forbids the use of social scoring AI systems evaluating individuals based on behavioral data resulting in unfair treatment.

Allows the Attorney General to enforce compliance and allows a private right of action for those harmed by AI non-compliance.

DOC_ID: 202
Establishes a framework for federal oversight of advanced AI models, termed "frontier models," to mitigate extreme AI-enabled risks like biological, chemical, cyber, and nuclear threats.

Defines frontier models as those requiring computing power exceeding 10^26 operations, and capable of diverse tasks or intended for specific high-risk areas.

Mandates entities selling or renting large computing resources to report usage and exercise due diligence, particularly concerning foreign clients.

Requires developers to notify an oversight entity before training frontier models and integrate safeguards against extreme risks.

Obligates developers to adhere to cybersecurity standards and report risk mitigation steps.

Imposes an evaluation and licensing process before deploying frontier models, using a tiered structure based on risk level to determine deployment scope.

Suggests options for an oversight entity, including an interagency body, new agency, or existing federal agencies, emphasizing expert involvement.

Tasks the oversight entity to report on emerging challenges and risks, ensuring framework adaptability.

DOC_ID: 204
Establishes principles and mechanisms for AI safety governance, emphasizing a balance between development and security and involving all stakeholders in a coordinated approach.

Advocates for international cooperation in AI safety governance, pursuing best practices and consensus building.

DOC_ID: 205
Highlights six key aspects for risk assessment of advanced AI systems: actionable, transparent, comprehensive, multistakeholder, iterative, and reproducible.

DOC_ID: 206
Establishes the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act to regulate high-risk AI systems and prohibit certain harmful AI uses.

Requires developers, distributors, and deployers of high-risk AI systems to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination and complete impact assessments.

Mandates the disclosure to consumers when interacting with high-risk AI systems and provides consumer rights to contest decisions made by these systems.

Prohibits AI systems from manipulating behavior, social scoring, using biometric identifiers without consent, inferring sensitive attributes, causing harm, and producing unlawful content.

Creates an Artificial Intelligence Council to guide ethical AI use, issue advisory opinions, and suggest legislative reforms.

Initiates an AI Regulatory Sandbox Program for controlled testing without full compliance.

Implements AI workforce development grants to address workforce gaps and support AI-related education.

Allows enforcement by the Attorney General with civil penalties for non-compliance.

Effective from September 1, 2025.

DOC_ID: 209
Amends state law to introduce Chapter 93M, mandating consumer protections in interactions with artificial intelligence (AI) systems.

Requires developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems to use reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination and disclose foreseeable risks.

Defines "high-risk AI systems" as those influencing consequential decisions in areas like education, employment, and healthcare.

Mandates developers to provide detailed documentation about AI system data usage, limitations, and discrimination mitigation measures.

Obligates deployers to establish risk management policies aligned with recognized standards, and perform annual impact assessments.

Demands transparency from deployers by notifying consumers before consequential decisions are made using high-risk AI systems, and offering appeal opportunities for adverse decisions.

Empowers the attorney general with exclusive enforcement authority, classifying violations as unfair trade practices.

Prohibits private rights of action, leaving enforcement to the state.

Allows attorney general to create rules for implementing these requirements, ensuring alignment with national standards.
