DOC_ID: 920
Mandates the board of trustees of the University of Tennessee, the board of regents, and local governing boards of state universities to adopt AI usage policies for students, faculty, and staff by July 1, 2025.

Encourages public higher education institutions to collaborate on policy development and implementation for AI use.

Requires these boards to post policies online and submit them to respective education committees by July 1, 2025, with noncompliance leading to mandatory reporting to the joint government operations committee.

Obliges the adoption of AI policies for students, teachers, and staff in schools by the 2024-2025 school year, with annual compliance reports due to the Department of Education starting July 1, 2024.

Instructs public charter schools to implement AI policies by the 2024-2025 school year, with annual compliance reporting to the Department of Education beginning July 1, 2024.

Takes effect immediately upon becoming law, citing public welfare.

DOC_ID: 921
Establishes the Talent Innovation Program in the Maryland Department of Labor to increase access to high-quality job training, with a focus on industries such as artificial intelligence.

Creates the Talent Innovation Fund as a special, nonlapsing fund to support the program, including job training and talent financing mechanisms.

Requires the program to conduct pilots, research, and stakeholder engagement to develop sustainable talent financing approaches.

Mandates an initial pilot program focused on the cybersecurity sector to expand regional cyber ranges for job training.

Authorizes the Department to provide grants and funding to various entities and individuals for training and support services.

Directs the State Treasurer to invest the fund's money, with interest earnings credited to the fund.

Compels the Department to adopt regulations and report annually on program activities and fund utilization to state leadership.

DOC_ID: 922
Defines "synthetic media" as manipulated images, audio, or video that create a false impression of a real individual's appearance, speech, or conduct.

Allows candidates to seek injunctive relief or damages if their appearance, speech, or conduct is altered using synthetic media in electioneering communications.

Requires disclosure for synthetic media, stating, "This (image/video/audio) has been manipulated," with specific guidelines for visibility and audibility.

Places the burden of proof on plaintiffs to demonstrate synthetic media use by clear and convincing evidence.

Encourages courts to expedite proceedings related to synthetic media disputes.

Holds sponsors, but not mediums, liable unless mediums alter content or remove required disclosures.

Exempts interactive computer services from being treated as publishers of third-party content, except under specific conditions.

Mandates the public disclosure commission to adopt rules supporting this chapter without constituting violations under existing campaign finance laws.

Create the Dyslexia Research Grant Program, administered by the State Department of Education, to support research on AI-based writing assistance for individuals with dyslexia.

Allow privately owned businesses in Nebraska researching AI writing tools for dyslexia to apply for grants, using a prescribed form.

DOC_ID: 924
Amends the Managed Care Reform and Patient Rights Act to require utilization review programs using algorithmic automated processes to ensure that only clinical peers make adverse determinations based on medical necessity.

Mandates these programs to register with the Department of Insurance every two years and provide proof of compliance with Health Utilization Management Standards or equivalent standards.

Requires licensed physicians with relevant board certifications to establish criteria for these automated processes and verify their accuracy.

Obliges health care plans to have appeals procedures, allowing appeals to be filed orally or in writing, with decisions rendered within specified timeframes.

Ensures confidentiality of patient-specific information obtained during utilization reviews, restricting access to authorized individuals.

Permits adverse determination appeals to an external independent review, with potential further appeals to the Department if found arbitrary or capricious.

Authorizes the Department to issue corrective actions or cease and desist orders for non-compliance.

DOC_ID: 925
Amends the penal law to include the unlawful dissemination or publication of intimate images created or altered by digitization.

Defines "digitization" as altering an image realistically using other persons' images or computer-generated images.

Requires intent to cause harm to the emotional, financial, or physical welfare of another person for the offense to occur.

Mandates that the actor knew or should have known that the person depicted did not consent to the dissemination or publication.

Repeals subdivision 2-a of section 245.15 of the penal law.

Specifies that the act takes effect on the sixtieth day after becoming law.

DOC_ID: 926
Amends the election law to require disclosure for political communications using materially deceptive media.

Mandates visual disclosures to be legibly printed, stating "This (image, video, or audio) has been manipulated."

Requires audio disclosures to be stated clearly at the beginning, end, and periodically for messages longer than two minutes.

Exempts satire, bona fide news reporting with clear disclosures, stations with consistent disclaimer requirements, and platforms disseminating third-party content in good faith.

Permits candidates depicted in deceptive media to seek legal relief, including court costs, attorney fees, and injunctions against unauthorized dissemination.

Grants courts authority to provide preliminary relief to plaintiffs likely to succeed in proving violations in upcoming elections.

Places the burden of proof on plaintiffs to establish use of deceptive media by clear and convincing evidence.

Clarifies that federal law takes precedence over state requirements for media labeling in political communications.

DOC_ID: 929
Prohibits entities in Washington D.C. from determining eligibility for, or access to information about, "important life opportunities" (e.g., housing or employment) on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, familial status, source of income, or disability in a discriminatory manner.

Requires entities to provide a public notice describing any algorithmic tools they use to determine eligibility for, or access to information about, important life opportunities. The notice must be sent to any individual about whom such a determination is made.

Requires the notice described above to cover the data collected by algorithmic tools and how that data is shared externally, how that data informs the tool's decisions, and the rights provided under this act.

Requires entities to provide notice, to any individual against whom they take any adverse action (e.g., firing) on the basis of an algorithmic determination, that the individual may access and submit corrections to any personal information considered by the algorithm. A human must then redo the decision based on the corrected information.  

Requires entities to audit their algorithmic decision-making tools before deployment and annually thereafter to analyze discriminatory effects and other impacts, and then implement reasonable measures to address findings of unlawful disparate impact. Entities must submit a report on the audit to the DC government.

Permits affected individuals or the DC Attorney General to file a civil action in response to violations of this law, with fines up to $10,000 per violation.
